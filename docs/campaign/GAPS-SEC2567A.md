# Section 2, 4–7 and Appendix A triage

## E-A4 — FINE-AS-PRINTED in the corrected draft

The preserved arXiv version is false for infinite sets: take A to be the entire graph and u ≡ 1; the exterior boundary is empty. The corrected draft already replaces the hypothesis by “nonempty finite subset” in [C2] (corrected.tex:826–832).

Proof of the corrected form: the mated-CRT graph is connected and infinite (successive integer vertices are adjacent). Every vertex of finite A has a path to Aᶜ. Choose a maximum M of u on A. If M exceeds every exterior boundary value, subharmonicity at any maximizer forces all its neighbors to have value M: their weighted average is at least M while none exceeds M. Following a path from a maximizer to Aᶜ gives a boundary vertex of value M, a contradiction. The argument does not require A to be connected. Empty A is excluded because the maximum is undefined.

Downstream checks: the discrete applications at corrected.tex:1042–1048, 1227, 1346–1351, 1382, 1516, 1640, 1817, 2124, 3002, 3062 and 3207–3211 use vertex sets inside a bounded ball or a finite discrete cluster, hence finite by local finiteness; empty domains make the comparison vacuous. The analogous Appendix A applications at 3460 and 3529 use its finite V. The continuum maximum principle at 1957 is independent. Thus [C2] suffices for these uses; this verdict does not establish unrelated hypotheses in those arguments.

## E-x2-2 — FALSE

At corrected.tex:1558–1564 the second equality asserts that the cluster is the graph closure of the positive odometer set. Take any 0 < t ≤ ε. The initial mass M = t/ε is in (0,1], so the initial configuration is already stable: no vertex topples, the odometer is identically zero, and the final positive-mass set is {0}. The closure of the positive odometer set is empty. This is a counterexample on every graph, independent of the random environment. The same issue affects the displayed identity at 1703–1705 and 2947–2951.

A proposed replacement for the cluster identity, for M > 0, is Dε(M) = {0} ∪ cl{v > 0}. Every toppled vertex retains positive mass and each neighbor receives positive mass; conversely each positive-mass vertex other than the source must have received mass from a toppled neighbor. For M > 1, the source topples, so it is already in the positive odometer set. Consequently the unmodified identity is valid in the fixed-t, ε → 0 applications once ε < t; this observation does not justify the displayed claim for all t and ε, nor the separate equality of stopped and unstopped odometers. These identities are formalized as `Sandpile.cluster_eq_insert_closure` and the counterexample as `Sandpile.cluster_of_mass_le_one`.

## E-c1-1 — FALSE

The last clause of Lemma 4.4 and the mass identity of Lemma 4.5 quantify over every t, ε > 0 (corrected.tex:1707–1723). Fix a realization with μh(Bρ) finite and η parametrized by μh area, and choose ε > μh(Bρ). The cell η([-ε,0]) contains 0 and has μh-mass ε, so it cannot be contained in Bρ; by continuity it meets ∂Bρ. Thus the source vertex 0 is outside the killed domain V = Vε(Bρ) \ Vε(∂Bρ). The killed Green function with source 0 is zero. The zero function is the least admissible supersolution for the resulting zero obstacle, and the discrete cluster and Laplacian are zero. Taking t = 2ε gives t/ε > 1 but 0 is not in the cluster; the mass sum is 0 rather than 2, even though the empty cluster satisfies the stated containment hypothesis. These realizations have probability one under the standing area parametrization and local finiteness assumptions.

A candidate correction is to require 0 ∈ V for these two conclusions. Continuity of η at 0 gives this for every sufficiently small ε, so the fixed-t small-mesh uses are compatible with the additional hypothesis. This candidate does not resolve the separate connectedness issue in Appendix A.

## E-c5-2 — MINIMAL-CORRECTION to Definition 6.2 and Lemma 6.3

At corrected.tex:2290 and 2306, allow arbitrary starting vertices a₁,…,aₖ ∈ Vε in place of a₁,…,aₖ ∈ Θ. Interpret P as the list or multiset of stopped walkers, preserving multiplicity. In particular, a walker started outside Θ settles immediately if its site is available, or is stopped immediately if the site is outside Θ′. The original statement is a special case; the generalization is used because the first starts in Lemma 6.10 are in cl Θ and subsequent starts are on the exterior stopping boundary.

Proof of the stronger stopped/restarted identity, conditional on the environment: let F(A,L) be the law of the final unstopped aggregate from occupied A and the list L of starting sites. IDLA has F(A,L) = F(A,L′) when L′ is a permutation of L: for two walkers, condition on their independent first exit sites from A. If those sites differ, both are added; if they coincide at b, one is added at b and the other continues with the transition law of a fresh walk from b, by the strong Markov property. The resulting kernel is symmetric in the two first exit sites; integrate and use adjacent transpositions. This exchangeability is proved as `aggValue_perm` in `MatedCRT/Proofs/Sec6/AbelianIDLA/Exchange.lean`.

Now let A ⊆ Θ′ be the current occupied set, P the list of walkers already stopped outside Θ′, and L the list still to release. Induct on L. At the end, releasing P has law F(A,P). For the next walker, if its first unoccupied site b lies in Θ′, both the unstopped process and the stopped process add b, and the induction applies to A ∪ {b}. If b lies outside Θ′, it is appended to P. Since b ∉ A, a fresh walker started at b is absorbed immediately; permutation invariance permits moving it to the front of P followed by the remaining starts. The strong Markov property gives the correct conditional law for each fresh continuation. Thus completing the stopped process and restarting P has law F(A,P ++ L). Starting with A = Θ and P empty proves the claimed identity. This induction, including arbitrary lists and multiplicities, is formalized by `runValue_stopped` and `isAbelianIDLA` in `MatedCRT/Proofs/Sec6/AbelianIDLA/Statement.lean:29` and `:83`.

Downstream check: the initial starts and every restart in Lemma 6.10 are now covered, as is the restarted expression in Lemma 6.3 itself. The additional radius containment needed to apply Lemma 6.9 during iteration is handled by using parameter (1+ρ)/2 and choosing the total radial expansion below (1−ρ)/4; this does not assert that the separate rate and shell estimates are proved.

## E-x3-2 — MINIMAL-CORRECTION to the sequence quantifier

Conditional proof of the corrected compactness claim: first fix t and an arbitrary deterministic sequence tending to zero. ## OdometerMeasurableSelectionStep — initial reduction

Pathwise existence of a Hölder limit can be converted into an environment-measurable choice if the following additional measurability is established. Let K be the closed ball, fₙ the rescaled least supersolutions, and let Hₘ,ⱼ be the compact set of functions on K with sup norm and Hölder constant at most m and exponent 1/j. Suppose that, for every n, m, j, the extended sup-norm distance d(fₙ,Hₘ,ⱼ) has a σ(h,η)-measurable version, and that the same is true for the distance to each member of a fixed countable dense subset of each Hₘ,ⱼ. The versions must agree simultaneously almost surely for the countably many choices.

Indeed, every Hölder function belongs to some Hₘ,ⱼ. For a sample with a convergent subsequence and Hölder limit, some Hₘ,ⱼ satisfies liminfₙ d(fₙ,Hₘ,ⱼ) = 0. Select the first such pair in a fixed enumeration, and recursively select indices with distance below 2⁻ᵏ. Compactness gives finite 2⁻ᵏ-nets of the selected Hₘ,ⱼ. At stage k, choose the first net ball of radius 2⁻ᵏ⁺¹ containing infinitely many of the previously retained indices, and retain only those indices. All choices use countable comparisons and are measurable. Taking successively increasing indices from these nested infinite sets produces a uniformly Cauchy subsequence; its limit lies in the closed set Hₘ,ⱼ and is Hölder. On the complementary event use a fixed increasing sequence. This establishes the selection conclusion under the stated distance-measurability hypothesis, without assuming pathwise relative compactness of the whole sequence.

The missing verification is material: `exists_measurable_rescaledMatedLeastSupersolution` (Proofs/Sec4/ConvergenceDivisible/MatedLeastMeasurable.lean:63) gives an ambient jointly measurable version, not the required σ(h,η)-measurable sup-norm distances. Joint measurability alone does not make a supremum over an uncountable spatial set measurable. In particular, the piecewise cell interpolants need not be continuous, so restricting the supremum to a deterministic dense set needs proof. This reduction therefore does not establish the assigned step or constitute a verdict.

## OdometerMeasurableSelectionStep — FINE-AS-PRINTED (paper-level argument)

The distance-measurability requirement in the preceding reduction can be met for the actual cell interpolants. This is a paper-level proof of the fixed-t, fixed-sequence selection step, not a claim that a Lean declaration proving the step already exists. It uses the standard mating-of-trees coupling and local finiteness of the map, both already part of the setting. The environmental versions of the curve and boundary-length path are supplied by `exists_envSigma_measurable_curve` and `exists_envSigma_measurable_bm` (Proofs/Sec3/UniformConvergenceGreenKernel/EnvironmentMeasurability.lean:13,44).

First fix one mesh. The boundary-length path determines the countable edge multiplicities by infima over compact intervals, and the curve determines whether each cell meets an open or closed ball. These quantities have σ(h,η)-measurable versions, simultaneously almost surely. The random walk transition probabilities and killed Green kernel are measurable functions of this countable graph and its vertex set, by summing the probabilities of finite paths and taking the increasing limit; hence so are the obstacle and reciprocal degrees. Almost surely the graph is locally finite and the two vertex sets defining the discrete obstacle problem are finite. On that event, the value of the least supersolution at a specified vertex is a Borel function of the countable data: strictify any admissible function by a small convex combination with a sufficiently large constant (the Laplacian threshold is strictly positive), then approximate the finitely many constraints and the chosen value by a member of a fixed countable dense family of functions on the vertices. Thus the infimum is determined by countably many measurable inequalities. The same strictification and approximation are proved as deterministic lemmas in Proofs/Sec4/ConvergenceDivisible/FiniteApproximation.lean; the ambient-measurable specialization is `aemeasurable_matedLeastSupersolution` (MatedLeastMeasurable.lean:12). Apply the countable-data construction to the environmental versions instead. Bad inputs can be assigned a fixed default on the measurable event that the finite and local-finiteness conditions fail; this does not affect the almost-sure identity. Repeating for the countably many meshes and vertices gives simultaneous environmental versions of all discrete values.

Second, let K be the closed ball and g a deterministic continuous function on K. The event that the sup norm of the interpolant minus g exceeds a rational threshold is a countable combination of measurable events, despite the interpolant's jumps. For a candidate least cell index a, the set of x ∈ K where a is selected is its compact cell with all lower-index cells removed. Properness of the curve bounds the relevant indices below on K; after fixing such a bound, only finitely many lower cells need be removed. A compact set minus finitely many closed cells is a countable union of compact sets obtained by requiring distance at least 1/m from their union. Intersecting with the compact sublevel or superlevel sets of g reduces a deviation to countably many compact-intersection tests. These tests are Borel in the continuous curve and the countable vertex values. This is the construction of `measurableSet_bounded_index_deviation` and `bounded_index_deviation_iff` (Proofs/Sec3/UniformConvergenceGreenKernel/DeviationMeasurable.lean:17,68), and it applies to any environmentally measurable vertex values, not only the Green kernel used later in that file. If the two margins and times are random, take a countable exhaustion by rational s, u and rational lower bounds on both margins, and localize using measurable events; choose the localization before sending ε to zero.

This route is not yet a proof of the printed result: the two continuum containment properties and the measurable localization for the actual regularized harmonic-ball family have not been checked. In particular, Hausdorff left continuity by itself does not say every interior point belongs to an earlier set. The unregularized obstacle clusters do have an earlier-time union identity (`cluster_eq_iUnion_earlier_rational`, HarmonicBalls/Proofs/Sec5/ClusterMassLeftContinuity.lean:20), but the family here is defined through interior of the closure, so that identity cannot be transferred without a separate argument. The second containment is precisely the strict-growth issue assigned to m1. The dependency graph lists Lemma 6.8, not Proposition 5.1, as an input to Proposition 7.1 (docs/campaign/GRAPH.md:72,79), so this route does not by itself create a 5.1 → 6.1 → 7.1 → 5.1 cycle; the individual corrected inputs must still be audited for hidden dependencies.

The first containment does not follow merely from strict growth, two-sided Hausdorff continuity, regular openness and continuous mass. For a concrete local deterministic counterexample, let μ be planar Lebesgue measure divided by π, put x = 1/2, and for 3/4 < s < 5/4 define `a(s) = max((1-s)/4, 0)` and `R(s) = sqrt(s + a(s)²)`. Take `A_s = B(0,R(s)) \ closedBall(x,a(s))` for s < 1 and `A_s = B(0,R(s))` for s ≥ 1. Each A_s is bounded, regular open, and contains 0; μ(A_s) = s. Both R(s) and −a(s) are strictly increasing on (3/4,1), and R(s) is strictly increasing thereafter, so `cl A_s ⊂ A_u` whenever s < u in this interval. The family is Hausdorff continuous: as s ↑ 1 the deleted disk shrinks to the point x and its Hausdorff distance from the full ball tends to zero. Nevertheless the compact set `{x}` lies in A_1 and in no earlier A_s. This family is not asserted to be a harmonic-ball family or to satisfy its obstacle characterization; it only rules out a proof of the needed containment from those abstract topological and mass properties alone. Some genuinely obstacle-specific property is necessary.

One such property is exactly the strict obstacle gap already isolated as E-x3-1. Fix t > 0 and choose one disk B_R containing Λ_t with a margin. By monotonicity the same disk contains every Λ_s for 0 < s < t. Write `C_s = cluster μ R 0 s`. The obstacle characterization gives `Λ_s = int(cl C_s)`, and under the local volume-growth and continuity hypotheses C_s is open (`isOpen_cluster_of_volumeGrowth`, HarmonicBalls/Proofs/Sec5/GeneralDomainClusterOpen.lean:62); hence C_s ⊆ Λ_s. Assume only at the fixed target time that `Λ_t ⊆ C_t`, i.e. the regularized cluster has strict obstacle gap away from the pole. For every x ∈ Λ_t, `mem_cluster_exists_earlier_rat` (HarmonicBalls/Proofs/Sec5/CountableTimeMeasurability.lean:30) gives a rational q ∈ (0,t) with x ∈ C_q ⊆ Λ_q. Conversely monotonicity gives Λ_q ⊆ Λ_t, so `Λ_t = ⋃_{q ∈ ℚ, 0<q<t} Λ_q`. Every compact K ⊂ Λ_t is covered by finitely many open Λ_q; monotonicity lets their maximum q cover all of K, proving the first containment required above. This uses the strict gap at t, not at all earlier times, but does not prove that gap. The strict-growth and measurable-localization obligations for the E-c1-5 route remain separate; this reduction is not an unconditional verdict.

The countable-localization obligation can also be discharged at the paper level, assuming the two containment properties, the local-cluster measurability input and the joint-law form of the discrete lower bound. Fix δ and t and work before the exit time. Set `A_v = int(cl(cluster μ ρ 0 v))` for v = t and for positive rational v < t; the obstacle characterization identifies A_v with Λ_v before exit. The point-membership events of A_v are null measurable (`LocalClusterMeasurable.lean:35`), and these sets are regular open. Let D be a countable dense subset of the plane. For any x, distance to A_v is the infimum over points of D ∩ A_v; distance to A_vᶜ is the infimum over points of D ∩ int(A_vᶜ), because regular openness makes int(A_vᶜ) dense in A_vᶜ. For d ∈ D, the event d ∈ int(A_vᶜ) is a countable union over rational r > 0 of the events that no point of D ∩ B_r(d) belongs to A_v. Thus both distance functions at each d ∈ D are null measurable.

For positive rational u < t and η > 0 define the null-measurable event that no d ∈ D satisfies either `(dist(d,A_tᶜ) > δ/2 and dist(d,A_uᶜ) < 2η)` or `(d ∈ A_u and dist(d,A_tᶜ) < 2η)`. The first condition, density and continuity of distance imply `B⁻_δ(A_t) ⊆ B⁻_{2η}(A_u)`; the second implies `cl A_u` has distance at least 2η from A_tᶜ, so its η-neighborhood is contained in A_t. Conversely, choose rational s < u < t with the compact set `B⁻_{δ/2}(A_t)` contained in A_s, then use strict growth `cl A_s ⊂ A_u` and `cl A_u ⊂ A_t` to get positive margins and choose rational η > 0 sufficiently small. This proves that the countable localization events cover the before-exit event up to a null set. Finite unions approximate that event in outer probability by continuity of the finite environment measure, using a measurable hull if the exit event itself is not known measurable.

On each localization, apply the joint-law discrete-cluster lower bound at the fixed deterministic time u and inner radius η (`joint_iDLALowerBound_discrete_of_steps`, Proofs/IDLA/JointDiscreteLowerBound.lean:13), and Proposition 7.1 at time u with outer radius η. The stopped discrete cluster is contained in the global sandpile cluster's graph closure by the stopped/global comparison, so its filled cells lie in the η-neighborhood of Λ_u on the latter good event; every such vertex belongs to V^ε(Λ_t). Coupling walkers by the same trajectories gives monotonicity of stopped IDLA in the stopping domain: if A ⊆ A' are the current occupied sets and D ⊆ D' are the stopping sets, a site newly occupied in the D-run either was already in A' or is also the first new site for the D'-run. Induction over walkers, followed by monotonicity in particle count, transfers the earlier-time lower event to the desired time-t stopped domain. A finite union of localized good events and then the outer-probability limit complete this conditional localization. The joint-law marginal and the graph stopped/global comparison must be instantiated with their actual hypotheses in any Lean proof; no new Lean declaration is claimed here. This leaves strict obstacle gap E-x3-1 and strict growth E-c5-3 as independent prerequisites, so it is still not an unconditional verdict on Proposition 5.1.

The comparison in the preceding paragraph can be sharpened and its hypotheses made explicit. On a locally finite, symmetric, loopless, connected graph of positive degrees, global stabilization supplies an admissible global normalized odometer U (`Sandpile.global_stabilization`, Proofs/Sandpile/Properties.lean:51). On the event that the stopped obstacle problem satisfies `IsMatedObstacleSetting` and its graph closure is contained in `cellVertices ε η (closedBall 0 ρ)`, comparison with the admissible supersolution U + φ gives the stopped odometer v ≤ U. This is the `hψ`/`hvle` argument in `Sandpile.obstacle_odometer_eq`, Proofs/Sandpile/Comparison.lean:57, and uses neither its later assumption that the global cluster is contained in the stopping domain nor equality of the two odometers. Thus the needed inclusion is into the global cluster itself, not merely its graph closure; the outer bound for filled global cells applies without an extra cell-diameter enlargement. The actual probability of all obstacle-setting and graph-geometry hypotheses must still be included in the final assembly; this paragraph is a paper-level extraction, not a new Lean theorem.

The environment marginal needed by the localization is already available with outer measures: `MatedCRTEnv.IsValid.idlaJointMeasure_env_le` (Proofs/IDLA/WalkerLawMeasurable.lean:133) bounds the joint measure of B × univ by S.P B for every B, and `idlaJointMeasure_le_add_env` at line 152 performs the required transfer. The preceding whole-domain obstacle-setting hypothesis is unnecessarily strong and should not be silently assumed: `ballInterior` can be disconnected (E-c1-2). ## E-c5-3 — horizon uniqueness: partial analysis (not a verdict)

Full strict growth is stronger than the condition needed for the deterministic horizon comparison. Suppose two increasing open families agree up to the smaller positive horizon, both satisfy `IsStoppedAtHalfBall`, and each has `closure(Λ_s) ⊂ B_{1/2}` for every s strictly before its own horizon. If T₁ < T₂, choose T₁ < s < T₂. The compact set `closure(Λ²_s)` lies in B_{1/2}, hence in some B_r with r < 1/2. Monotonicity and agreement put every Λ¹_v, v < T₁, in B_r, contradicting its stopping condition. Interchanging the families gives T₁ = T₂. This is the same argument as `IsStoppedAtHalfBall.le_of_eqOn` (Steps/Sec2/ConeHarmonicBallsUpToFixedTime.lean:226), with `ConeHalfBallClosureInsideStep` (Proofs/Sec2/ConeFamilyMeasurable/StoppedClosure.lean:20) replacing its use of strict growth. The weaker property itself is not proved here or supplied merely by the stopping condition.

Indeed, the set-theoretic and mass conditions alone permit different horizons even with genuinely increasing sets. Let μ be planar Lebesgue measure divided by π. For 0 < t < 0.16 put r = √t, c = min(2r/3, 1/2 − r), and A_t = B(c,r), viewing c as a real point in ℂ. Then 0 ∈ A_t, μ(A_t) = t, and each A_t is bounded, connected and regular open. For r ≤ 0.3 the center moves by 2/3 of the radius increment; for r ≥ 0.3 it moves left at exactly the radius increment. Consequently A_s ⊊ A_t whenever s < t, with Hausdorff continuity and shrinking to {0} as t ↓ 0. Every A_t lies in B_{1/2}; for t ≥ 0.09 its closure touches 1/2, so for every T with 0.09 < T < 0.16 the same family restricted to (0,T) satisfies `IsStoppedAtHalfBall`. Thus two different horizons agree on their common interval. This does not refute Lemmas 2.4–2.5: the sets are not harmonic balls centered at 0 for μ, since the mean of the harmonic function Re z over an off-center disk is c rather than 0. It demonstrates precisely why an obstacle-specific or harmonic-ball-specific argument is needed to rule out premature boundary contact; the source and proof of that argument remain open.
