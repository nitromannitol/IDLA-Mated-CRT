## Quantifiers and normalization

For each `0 < γ < 2`, `0 < ρ < 1`, valid environment `S`, disk `D = ball z r ⊆ ball 0 ρ`, and deterministic `y ∈ D`, the environment event may depend on these parameters. On that event, the exit time is finite for almost every Brownian path started at `y`, and the equality holds **simultaneously for every** bounded Hölder `f` on `D`. The point `y = 0` is included whenever 0 belongs to `D`. The integrals in the Lean statement are real-valued Bochner integrals, so finiteness and measurability must be proved before applying nonnegative potential identities. `greenFunction` is normalized by `ΔG = −δ`; standard planar Brownian motion has generator `Δ/2`, hence its killed Green kernel is `2G`.

`S.IsValid` gives a single environment event on which the dyadic clock approximations converge in Brownian probability locally uniformly in time for **all** starting points (`MatedCRT/Defs/Setting.lean:155`, `HarmonicBalls/Defs/LBM.lean:89`). It separately gives dyadic vague convergence of the area measures (`HarmonicBalls/Defs/LQG.lean:53`). This all-start assumption is stronger than convergence for one start; it must not be discarded in a proposed counterexample.

## Checked sources and their scope

* Garban, Rhodes and Vargas, [*Liouville Brownian motion*](https://sites.math.duke.edu/~rtd/readgroup/Louiville.pdf), Ann. Probab. 44 (2016), Theorem 2.7(1)–(4), pp. 3085–3086: for their massive GFF and specified regularizations, almost surely there is a PCAF with Revuz measure their Liouville measure; for every start the approximating clocks converge locally uniformly in probability, and the limiting clock is strictly increasing and unbounded almost surely. The theorem does not state the same assertion for the circle-average cone or identify its particular regularization with theirs. Their Theorem 2.2 and Proposition 2.3, pp. 3081–3084, use local power bounds on measures to control logarithmic Green potentials; those hypotheses and conclusions cannot simply be assumed for this cone's approximations.
* Berestycki, [*Diffusion in planar Liouville quantum gravity*](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.1214/14-AIHP605.pdf), Ann. Inst. H. Poincaré 51 (2015), Theorem 1.2, p. 949, constructs the zero-boundary-GFF diffusion using dyadic circle averages, for `0 ≤ γ < 2`; Section 2, pp. 950–954, proves convergence of the clock. This is not a killed-potential/Revuz theorem for the cone.
* Gwynne and Miller, [*The Tutte embedding of the Poisson–Voronoi tessellation of the Brownian disk*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00220-019-03610-5), Section 2 (quantum cones): the restriction of the circle-average embedding of an `α`-quantum cone to the unit disk has the law of a whole-plane GFF normalized on the unit circle plus `−α log|·|`. For this task `α = γ`. This identifies the marked singularity; it does not itself identify the PCAF or supply a potential identity at 0.

## Conditional reduction and the unresolved bridge

Fix the parameters and `D,y`. If, for almost every environment, a Brownian PCAF `A` with Revuz measure `S.μ ω` has (i) the same law/pathwise version as `S.clock ω` under `planarBrownianMotion y` through the exit time, and (ii) the killed potential formula at this particular `y`, including finite `∫_D G_D(y,x) μ(dx)`, then the result follows. Brownian exit `T_D` is almost surely finite; a continuous strictly increasing unbounded clock maps it to the LBM exit time `A(T_D)`, which is finite. The pathwise Stieltjes substitution gives

`∫₀^{A(T_D)} f(B_{A⁻¹(t)}) dt = ∫₀^{T_D} f(B_s) dA_s`.

The killed potential formula gives expectation `∫_D 2 G_D(y,x) f(x) μ(dx)` first for nonnegative Borel `f`. The finite potential gives integrability for every bounded `f`; split signed `f` into positive and negative parts. A single equality of the corresponding finite occupation measures gives all bounded Hölder tests simultaneously, rather than taking an uncountable intersection of null events. A formal proof also has to establish the required measurable representatives and identify Lean's real integrals with these finite expectations.

This is a conditional reduction, not a proof that (i)–(ii) hold. A generic Revuz correspondence typically identifies potentials only outside an exceptional set unless an all-start version and its pointwise potential formula are justified. The singular field at 0 precludes simply transferring a result by local absolute continuity on the entire disk. An exhaustion by annuli must also control the clock and Green-potential contributions near 0, in particular when the Brownian path starts there. The other missing comparison is between massive-GFF approximations and the exact dyadic circle averages and their selected limits; the cone-law statement alone does not establish it.

## Refute-first check

The following shows why the two separate convergence clauses, at a **single** start, are insufficient; it is not a counterexample to `S.IsValid`. Fix `a ≠ y`, let `φ ≥ 0` be smooth, supported in the unit disk with unit integral, and set `w_n(x) = 1 + n²φ(n(x−a))`. The measures `w_n(x) dx` converge vaguely to `dx + δ_a`. A planar Brownian path from `y` almost surely does not hit `a`; its compact range over any fixed finite interval has positive distance from `a`, so `∫₀ᵗ w_n(B_s) ds = t` for all sufficiently large `n`, uniformly over bounded time intervals. The limiting identity clock has Lebesgue, not `dx + δ_a`, as occupation measure. The densities can be encoded by the formulas in the definitions at dyadic scales. However these artificial fields are not quantum-cone samples, and the all-start clause is not established at `a`; consequently this example does not refute the assigned proposition.
