## N3 — random subsequence in Lemma 3.1

**Verdict: FALSE for the Borel–Cantelli inference at line 1018; the lemma itself is not refuted.** Let U be uniform on (0,1), and call ε bad exactly when ε = U/n for some positive integer n. For every deterministic ε, the probability of failure is zero, yet the σ(U)-measurable sequence U/n consists entirely of bad scales. In fact it contains no nonempty deterministic infinite subsequence almost surely. Thus neither polynomial fixed-scale bounds nor Borel–Cantelli justify the displayed claim for the random initial sequence. This counterexample concerns the inference, not the mated-CRT estimates themselves.

A valid limited repair is: first fix a deterministic sequence εn → 0; thin it deterministically so the failure probabilities of the finitely many required estimates sum to less than ∞; Borel–Cantelli gives an event on which they hold eventually, including along every subsequently chosen random subsequence of that thinned sequence. This does not establish the printed lemma for an arbitrary random initial sequence, and replacing its statement requires an audit of every use and quantifier order. Alternatively one needs an estimate uniform over all sufficiently small ε, or an independently proved summable covering of the random sequence. Neither follows from the stated fixed-scale probability bounds. The deterministic-subsequence route in Lemma 3.5 lines 1323–1359 is compatible with the limited repair if the good-scale thinning is made before its random compactness subsequence; this observation is not a complete audit of all other uses.

## E-m2-1 — exit-time tails in Lemma 3.4

**Verdict: MINIMAL-CORRECTION to the proof, subject to the separately identified fixed-time and measurability steps.** The first-moment argument at lines 1162–1171 is invalid: for Z_n=n with probability 1/n and 0 otherwise, E Z_n=1 but sup_n E[Z_n 1{Z_n>T}]=1 for every T. Replace it by the N=2 case of Lemma 2.18 (lines 919–932), together with mε ≍ ε⁻¹ and Lemma 2.17. For a fixed disk D=B_r(z), its LQG mass is positive almost surely, and an enlarged ball containing D lies in B_ρ' for some ρ<ρ'<1. The enlarged logarithmic potential is finite a.s. by Lemma 2.17. Consequently there is a finite random K such that, with probability tending to one, E[(τε/mε)² | h,η] ≤ K. This is the bound formalized in `Proofs/Sec3/IntegralConvergenceGreenFunction/UniformSecondMoment.lean:14`. Hence the conditional contribution after time T is at most ||f||∞ K/T (up to the vanishing one-step interpolation error), in probability; if ||f||∞ is random, it is tight and the same conclusion follows by localization.

The continuum mean exit time also is finite a.s. without first assuming the Revuz formula: choose a disk D' with cl D ⊂ D' ⊂ B_ρ', apply the same first-moment bound to its discrete exit times, and use the vanishing cell diameter to bound the interpolated path's exit time from D by the discrete exit time from D' plus one mesh-time. For any deterministic εn↓0, thin to a subsequence along which quenched path laws converge weakly a.s. and the needed good events and cell bounds hold eventually. Exit time from an open set is lower semicontinuous on continuous paths in the compact-open topology: every path segment strictly before exit stays a positive distance from the complement. Portmanteau applied to τ_D∧T therefore gives E[τ_D∧T | h,η] ≤ sqrt(K') a.s., with K' finite independent of T. Monotone convergence gives E[τ_D | h,η] < ∞. The arbitrary-sequence/subsequence criterion yields the a.s. conclusion. Thus the continuum tail tends to zero by dominated convergence. Combining both tail bounds with the fixed-T convergence gives the required limit in probability; it does not assert an event good at every small mesh. This argument uses the independently required quenched path-law convergence, cell comparison and measurability; it does not resolve E-c2-9 or IR-m2-1.

## E-c2-9 — environment-dependent test functions

**Verdict: MINIMAL-CORRECTION to the proof at lines 1139–1150; no new convergence input is needed for this dependence.** A bounded Hölder f on D extends continuously to K=cl D. Fix a continuous retraction R:ℂ→K. Since C(K) is separable, choose a countable sup-norm-dense family of bounded Lipschitz functions g_j on K. For every δ>0 and environment, some g_j satisfies ||f−g_j||∞<δ. The sets {||f−g_j||∞<δ} are measurable: the norm is the supremum over a countable dense subset of K, provided f is jointly measurable (as the paper's random-function language requires). Since these sets cover the probability space, finitely many cover all but an arbitrarily small probability. Select the first applicable j on that finite union.

For each deterministic g_j the path test F_j(w)=∫₀ᵀ g_j(R(w_t))dt is bounded Lipschitz for the compact-open path metric after a deterministic scaling, so Theorem 2.11 applies. For either conditional path law, replacing f∘R by g_j∘R changes its expectation by at most Tδ. Thus the error for the random f is bounded by 2Tδ plus the maximum of finitely many deterministic-test errors, outside the small exceptional event. Let ε→0, then δ and the exceptional probability tend to zero. The same argument works for bounded random sup norm, by localization. This gives convergence in probability for the un-killed fixed-T functional under the stated conditional-law and measurability hypotheses. The comparison to the sum killed on exiting D needs the separate exit-time/window argument and regularity at exit; this proof does not supply it. Likewise, the Lean proposition `LBMPathFunctionalMeasurableStep` is a separate requirement and is not implied merely by pointwise convergence of deterministic tests.
